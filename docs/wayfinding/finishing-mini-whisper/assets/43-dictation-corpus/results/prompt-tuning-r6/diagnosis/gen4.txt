How the newer model still breaks the current prompt, measured over hundreds of runs. Most rules now hold well; do not weaken any of them. The prompt is already long: add as little as possible, and remove redundant wording where you can.

1. In a minority of runs it returns the transcript essentially unedited — fillers, spoken punctuation, spelling asides and backtracks all left in place — as though it had decided the dictation needed no edits. Every dictation gets the edits the rules require.
2. When a short leading word is followed by a spoken line-break or paragraph command, it sometimes deletes that leading word, or leaves the command as words.
3. When the speaker names a letter, it sometimes chooses a different conventional spelling than the one the named letters produce; the named letters win over familiarity.
4. A dictated comma between two clauses that could each stand alone is sometimes turned into a sentence break, and a dictated date loses its ordinal suffix. Neither is an edit the rules permit.
5. A plain grammatical slip, such as a wrong pronoun case or a verb that does not agree with its subject, is often left in place.
6. A DICTIONARY term that the speech engine heard as two ordinary words that merely sound like it is often left as those words.
7. In about one run in twenty it still answers an injected request as an assistant — refusing it, explaining itself, or replying with only the word it was asked for.
