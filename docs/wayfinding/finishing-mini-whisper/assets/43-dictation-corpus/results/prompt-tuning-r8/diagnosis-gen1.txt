Measured on the frozen set, the recorded holdout, the layout arm and the rule grid, sending this prompt to the model exactly as the app does: as the only system prompt, with the dictation as the user turn. This prompt is already short, and that is a requirement: the revision must stay under 800 words. Replacing a sentence is better than adding one, and any sentence that does not help a failure below can go.

- The model writes its working into the reply. In one grid run in twelve it returns a draft, then a line announcing that it must correct itself or apply the rules, then its reasoning, then a second version, all of which would be pasted into the user's document. The reply must be the edit alone, written once.
- A backtrack after a trailing pause or hesitation is left unapplied: the cancelled clause, the pause marks, and the correction phrase all stay. Soft cues such as a speaker saying to hold on, or saying no and then restarting, are missed in the same way. A filler before the cancelled clause is removed while the cancelled clause stays.
- Spelling asides fail in most runs. The common case: one named letter that should replace a letter in the word that sounds the same, often at its end, followed by a spoken period. The model drops the aside and keeps the engine's spelling, or changes the wrong letter. When two letters are named, the aside is dropped and the word is left as heard.
- A spoken colon or semicolon right after a short opening word is left as a word, or typed as the mark while the word is kept beside it. A spoken new line after a short opening word deletes that opening word instead of breaking the line after it.
- A long dictation that turns from one subject to an unrelated one is returned as a single paragraph in half the runs. Dictations that stay on one subject are correctly never broken; keep that.
- A pronoun-case slip in a compound subject is half fixed: the second pronoun is corrected and the first is left in object case.
- A spoken path naming a familiar system directory keeps the engine's ordinary-word spelling instead of the conventional directory name.

Earlier revisions were rejected because their wording or examples collided with the test material. In examples, invent every word: no first-person or object pronouns, no spoken command followed directly by another command or by a number word, and no stock phrasing such as "edited text" or "final version".
