 - remind me why AudioInputDevice and MicrophoneSelection must be 2 separate entities


 Thinking...

 Different tense. One is a durable intent, the other an observation with a lifetime of one snapshot.

 MicrophoneSelection has to survive the thing it names. Its whole point is being able to say "the Studio Mic" while the Studio Mic is in a drawer — that is
 what makes the fallback heal instead of overwrite. An AudioInputDevice? cannot express that: nil would mean both "user chose system default" and "the device
 is gone," and those must stay distinct or the selection gets rewritten the first time someone unplugs something.

 It also carries .systemDefault, which is not a device at all. "Whatever macOS considers current" has no UID until you ask.

 And the fields do not survive equally. uid is durable; transport and visibility are true only of a device that is plugged in right now. Folding an
 AudioInputDevice into the stored selection would persist a claim about hardware that the hardware is free to contradict — and then isExplicitlySelectable
 would have to choose between trusting the fossil or re-reading, at which point the stored copy is dead weight. lastKnownName is the one field deliberately
 allowed to go stale, because nothing but a sentence in the UI reads it, and the snapshot heals it when the device returns.

 So: AudioInputDevice is what CoreAudio says exists. MicrophoneSelection is what the user asked for. AudioInputRoute.resolving is the only place the two meet.
