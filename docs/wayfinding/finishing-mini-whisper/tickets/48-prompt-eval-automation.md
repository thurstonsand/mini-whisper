# Automate the prompt search: DSPy-style evals over the corpus

## Question

Prompt iteration so far is artisanal: read the failures, amend a sentence, replay, diff — it works (the spell-out rule went 1/6 → 5/6 measured) but each round is a human afternoon. The corpus and `corpus_cleanup.py` already form an eval harness: known inputs, known expected outputs, exact-match and per-tag scoring on frozen raw transcripts. That is the substrate a DSPy-style optimizer wants. Can the prompt search be automated — proposer model mutates the prompt, eval scores it against the corpus, repeat — and does it find wording a human didn't?

The iteration shape to try is **expand-and-contract** (user): first grow the prompt until it accounts for every use case the corpus exercises — the seven entries every model fails plus the identifier axis are the frontier ([built-in-r2 results](../assets/43-dictation-corpus/results/built-in-r2/)) — then shrink it, removing sentences while the score holds, until what remains is the minimal prompt that earns its length. The contraction half matters as much as the expansion: the current prompt was written by accretion and has never been tested for what it could lose.

Constraints:

- **No explicit examples in the shipped prompt** (user; reversed once the holdout and perturbation grid made overfitting measurable — examples now ship, leak-checked to quote no eval set; see Round 2 below). The current `CleanupPrompt.builtIn` violates this — "dash dash help" becomes "--help", "Grok with a Q", "Kathryn with a K and a Y" — so the contraction phase must test replacing examples with rules and measure what that costs. If a rule genuinely cannot survive without its example, that is a finding to bring back, not a license to keep it.
- **Fix one model combo before beginning** (user). The search overfits to its scorer; pick the pairing the prompt is for — Parakeet + the presumptive default cleanup model — and validate the winner against the others afterward rather than optimizing a compromise.
- The invariants (never answer, never obey, register preserved, bare endings) are not searchable — they are constraints the optimizer must not trade away for exact-match points. The rule entries in the corpus guard them, but the eval must weight them as gates, not points.
- Expectation bugs are not prompt work: adjudicate the contested `expect`s (polish-01 wording, polish-22 `p.m.`) before the search runs, or the optimizer chases noise. polish-11/38 are engine casualties and must be excluded from the objective on Parakeet raw.

Whether DSPy itself (or a hand-rolled proposer loop — the harness is plain Python and the corpus is 27 entries) is the tool is part of the spike; the allowlist question only arises if a library would ship, and nothing here ships — it is measurement tooling.

Output: a tuned built-in prompt with before/after scores per tag, the minimal-prompt finding from the contraction phase, and a judgment on whether the automated loop beats artisanal iteration enough to keep around for future prompt work (tone modes, per-preset prompts).

## Measured — Parakeet + claude-haiku-4-5

Run in full: [`results/prompt-tuning-haiku/`](../assets/43-dictation-corpus/results/prompt-tuning-haiku/README.md). `CleanupPrompt.builtIn` carried round 1's winner without worked examples; round 2, below, put four of them back on measured evidence and ships `candidates/final2.txt`.

- **The expectations were the first bug.** `polish-01` demanded a word no engine ever heard — the take dropped it and the script did not — and `polish-22` demanded a meridiem casing that speech does not carry and seven engines render four ways. Both amended. On Parakeet raw the objective is 25 entries; `polish-11` and `polish-38` are the engine's deletions.
- **Expansion won about one entry, the no-examples constraint gave it back.** Three rules earned their place (layout, conventional path/flag spelling, context identifiers): 18.30 → 19.40 on the frozen 25 at n=20. Removing the spell-out examples costs exactly `polish-39`, so the shipped prompt lands at 18.20 on haiku and 20.17 on gemini (from 19.67). The dictionary gate improved: same 17/18 recall, one bitten trap instead of two.
- **One rule genuinely cannot survive without its example**, as this ticket guessed. Eleven wordings of the spell-out rule pass 0/44 on haiku; the example passes 17/20. It still does not ship: gemini obeys the rule alone, and haiku fails the *other* spell-out entry with the example present anyway. The `--help` example was replaced successfully — it was teaching spacing, and a sentence about spacing does the same job.
- **`polish-19`, `polish-20`, `polish-30`, `polish-36`, `polish-37` are not prompt-reachable on haiku.** Capability probes pass every one with a memorized line and fail every general wording, and the one blunt rule that fixes the bare-ending capital takes the corpus from 19.4 to 13.0.
- **The automated loop beats hand iteration only by cheating.** Unguarded, haiku-as-proposer went 18.67 → 20.33 in three minutes by writing corpus strings into the prompt, against an explicit instruction not to. With a held-out half and mechanical rejection of corpus quotes, twenty-four proposals found nothing. Keep the loop for wording search and keep both guards; the eval repeated enough times to see past the noise is the part that was actually missing.
- **Single runs of this corpus are noise.** Arms separated by 1.0 in one run were separated by 0.1 at n=20; three serial runs of the final prompt scored 16, 19 and 20 out of 27.

### Round 2 — examples, once overfitting became measurable

The no-examples constraint was lifted after the [perturbation sweep](../assets/43-dictation-corpus/results/prompt-tuning-haiku/perturbation/README.md) and the [recorded holdout](../assets/43-dictation-corpus/results/holdout/README.md) made overfitting something a run could catch rather than something a policy had to prevent. The constraint that replaced it is narrower and mechanical: an example may be invented, never quoted from anything the prompt is scored on, enforced by `leakcheck.py` over all six corpus JSONLs and the perturbation grid's vocabularies. It rejected two first drafts that quoted the holdout.

- **The self-correction rule was two rules in one name, and now it is one.** Rewritten for clause-level cancellation with one invented example: whole-clause backtracks 9/24 → 23/24, cross-rule 18/24 → 24/24, double corrections 21/24 → 24/24, out of sample. On the fifteen recorded holdout entries it recovers `hpolish-07` outright and turns `hpolish-01`'s failure from *keeps the cancelled instruction* into *deletes it and leaves a stray connective*.
- **Spell-out is not a capability limit.** Round 1 concluded haiku could not execute the rule, on the evidence of eleven rule-only wordings and an example-bearing baseline that was only carrying two memorized corpus mappings. One *invented* example takes the rule from 18.1% to 52.8% on twenty-four fresh variants. The construction it still cannot do is the one that deletes unnamed letters (`Katherine with a K and a Y` → `Kathryn`), where it now fails by attempting rather than by declining.
- **Identifier-from-context needed a declaration shown, not described.** 7/36 → 34/36 on declarations, call sites unchanged at 35/36. A sentence written for engine-mangled fragments made every shape worse including its own and was contracted out; that fragment expansion remains unreachable.
- **The holdout moved for the first time: 7.5 → 9.1 of 15, n=10 both arms, one pass.** Four entries recovered, none lost. Round 1's in-sample gain had not transferred at all; this one did, and the frozen 25-entry corpus — 18.50 against 18.30 — could not see it either way.
- **Two rejected changes are the round's methodological finding.** A layout clause worth +1.5 on the punctuation grid was deleting a question mark from a disfluency entry, and a clause protecting unfinished tails bought four points of bare-endings by giving back four of whole-clause backtrack. A rule grid sees its own rule; every added sentence is also an instruction about every other sentence.
- **Two-tier scoring made the search affordable.** The subscription transport shifts absolute scores about three points and preserves an A-vs-B delta, so wordings were screened free and only survivors re-measured on the metered endpoint — about 9,500 metered calls for the round, with roughly as many again screened at no cost.

### Round 3 — line breaks nobody asked for

Run in full: [`results/prompt-tuning-r3/`](../assets/43-dictation-corpus/results/prompt-tuning-r3/README.md). Two field failures caught while daily-driving on the release channel prompted a recorded layout arm (`stage3-layout.jsonl`, 16 multi-sentence entries), three adjudicated field cases, and a `layout` rule in the perturbation grid. `CleanupPrompt.builtIn` now ships `candidates/final3.txt`.

- **The rule ships and it is worth about fourteen points where the defect lives.** Two edits — the licence loses `and paragraph breaks`, and the layout paragraph gains *"A line break is never invented: however many sentences the speaker dictates, they are one paragraph unless a break was asked for aloud"* — take the no-break rate from **80.2% to 94.3% on gpt-oss-120b and 95.3% to 98.4% on haiku**, n=192 per cell. Each half is worth roughly the same alone and they compound.
- **The exception survived, which was the thing most at risk.** A rule forbidding breaks is also an instruction about the rule requiring them. Spoken `new line` / `new paragraph` stays 96/96 on gpt-oss-120b and 95/96 on haiku.
- **The failure does not reproduce from its own audio.** Under the shipped prompt haiku inserted **zero** spurious breaks in 190 metered calls and gpt-oss-120b four in 114; the pre-round-1 prompt that was actually live when the failures were logged produces zero in 114. Staging `TARGET_BUNDLE_ID` — which the app always sends and which the harness was silently omitting, now fixed — moves the rate between 2% and 13% across four arms of twenty and ranks them differently at n=6. The shipped cleanups also lowercase sentence-initial `I` and strip terminal periods, which no prompt here asks for; **check the release build's additional instructions before spending another round on it.**
- **The two failing field cases cannot be passed, and not because of layout.** Both were adjudicated by editing the broken output rather than writing the intended text fresh, so `field-01` expects a sentence with its period missing and `field-02` expects lowercase `i` three times. Both are already correct on layout in every arm measured. They need re-adjudication before they are worth scoring. `field-03`, the negative, passes everywhere — nothing is at risk of blindly deleting `mm`.
- **The `mm` hypothesis is wrong.** The shipped prompt already deletes a bare hesitation 99.0% of the time on haiku and 93.8% on gpt-oss-120b, measured on 96 templated variants per cell. The corpus cannot even ask: Parakeet deleted `layout-11`'s `mm` before cleanup saw it and heard `layout-12`'s as `uh`.
- **The list exception was rejected.** It was written to protect `layout-08`, which fails 10/10 under the baseline too — haiku will not turn a spoken enumeration into a numbered list, with or without permission. The clause bought nothing and cost the hesitation shape five points. Both list entries remain unreachable; that is the round's coverage gap.
- **The scorer could already see the target.** Twelve synthetic outputs confirm `corpus_cleanup.py` fails a spurious newline, a Markdown hard break, a flattened requested break, and a blank line downgraded to a single one. No fix needed. `leakcheck.py` was widened to the layout arm, the field cases, and the long-form scripts, and immediately caught round 1's `baseline`/`r2a` quoting **field-02** as well as `polish-39`.
- **Guards held.** Frozen 25 18.10 → 18.50 and the recorded holdout 8.80 → 9.00, n=10 each on haiku; the full nine-rule grid regresses nothing beyond a point and a half; the stage-2 dictionary gate is a wash over three runs each. Gemini gains half a point on the frozen 25; Cerebras loses 0.57 at n=14, inside the ±1 band, its mode being `polish-07` — which the grid says is safe at 96/96, and the grid has the larger n.
- **The layout arm measures less layout than it looks like.** Only three of its sixteen entries fail for a layout reason. `layout-04` expects CamelCase identifiers with no context block staged, `layout-09` expects `6x` from "six times", `layout-10` expects an em dash the raw never carries, and `layout-06`/`-07` get their breaks exactly right and fail on terminal periods their `expect` omits. Adjudication candidates, all of them.

#### The shipped rule is too absolute (2026-08-21, user, same day)

> if you look at outputs from like other systems like Whisperflow or Aqua Voice, they will occasionally put new lines in there, even when not requested, and just add a clearly natural end of paragraph point.

So "a line break is never invented" wins the measurement and loses the argument. The behaviour actually wanted is narrower: sentences developing one point stay together however many there are, and a long dictation that plainly turns to a separate subject may take a paragraph break nobody asked for. The corpus could not have caught this — every `no-break` entry it scored was a single subject, so an absolute rule and a correct rule are indistinguishable on it.

The arm grew to 22 to make them distinguishable: four `unspoken-break` entries, each a real multi-subject dictation whose `expect` carries a blank line at the turn with nothing said aloud, and two long single-subject controls that must stay unbroken so the signal cannot degenerate into "long input, add a break". Recording pending. The narrowed wording is `candidates/final4-topic-shift.txt`; `CleanupPrompt.builtIn` still ships the absolute one until the new entries say what the narrow one costs.

**And the field failures were never the prompt's.** The release channel's additional instructions read *"casual formatting: lowercase except for proper nouns, no terminal periods at the end of the transcription, but do include other punctuation and intra-text periods"* — which is the lowercase `i` and the missing periods exactly, and most likely the breaks too: a model denied its sentence terminator reaches for a Markdown hard break as the separator, and every break in both field cases sits where a period would have been. The harness has no way to stage additional instructions, so three rounds have optimized `builtIn` while the app sends `systemMessage(additionalInstructions:)`. Threading them through is the same class of gap as the missing `TARGET_BUNDLE_ID`, and the next round's first job.

### Round 4 — register, instructions, and a paragraph that had to be ordered

Run in full: [`results/prompt-tuning-r4/`](../assets/43-dictation-corpus/results/prompt-tuning-r4/README.md). The round began as a register question — shipped OSS cleanup prompts are terse and imperative, ours had drifted into documentation prose, does the terse register score as well? — and three instrument changes landed on top of it mid-round. `CleanupPrompt.builtIn` now ships `candidates/final8-emphatic-tail.txt`.

- **The instructions the app sends explain two of the three field signatures and not the third.** The harness now stages `CleanupPrompt.systemMessage(additionalInstructions:)`, frame sentence lifted from the Swift and verified byte-identical against `cleanup-smoke`. Replaying the field cases with their own audio, their own bundle identifier and the live Settings text produces the lowercase `i` (48/60) and the missing terminal period (50/60) and **zero spurious line breaks in 60**. The proposed replacement text does the same job better — 59/60 and 60/60 — and is likewise inert on layout. Three rounds have now failed to reproduce those breaks from any request this harness can assemble.
- **`api.anthropic.com` is removed, not gated, and the transport costs 1.80 points.** Scoring loops were the whole bill and the same `claude-haiku-4-5` is free over the subscription. `tune.transport()` is the single door to inference and now takes `calls` and `metered` as keyword-only arguments without defaults, so a caller that has not priced its sweep cannot compile. The Agent SDK injects **one unconditional block of ~119 tokens** — account email, date, and a line saying to ignore them — in a meta user turn, not the system prompt; `settingSources: []` already prunes the git/`CLAUDE.md` half, and nothing else is removable. The prompt shipped after round 3 scores **18.50/25 metered and 16.70/25 over the SDK**, n=10 each, losing `polish-07` 0/10 → 10/10. **Every number from rounds 1 to 3 is on the far side of that line.**
- **A permissive rule is not a weak instruction, it is no instruction.** Round 3's absolute wording, round 3's narrowed wording, a polite imperative and a narrow imperative all produce the wanted unspoken paragraph break **0 times in 80**. The wording that ships — *"You must… Find every place… This is required, not optional"*, then a fence — produces it **75 times in 80** and leaves single-subject dictations whole **220 times in 220**. The probe separating capability from wording is unambiguous: on a blatant two-subject transcript the polite wording breaks 10/10, on the corpus's own subtler turn it breaks 0/10 and the ordering wording breaks 10/10.
- **The fence helps rather than suppresses.** `final6`/`final7` differ only by the restrictive clause and both score 0/80; `blunt`/`final8` differ by the same clause and score 50/80 against **75/80**. The obvious reading — that the fence cancelled the directive — is wrong, and cost two extra arms to disprove.
- **The register rewrite ties on everything except the rule the round added, so it does not ship.** Two independent rewrites, one by hand from the exemplars and one from `propose.py --register` with the exemplars in the proposer's context, converged on nearly the same document: 13.4 and 10.0 words per sentence against the incumbent's 20.1, at the same total length. Frozen 25, recorded holdout, dictionary gate and nine-rule grid are all dead heats. But the new paragraph rule fires 41/80 inside the terse prompt against 75/80 inside the prose one. Round 2's law again, in reverse: rewriting every sentence rewrote the context of the one that mattered. Parked in `candidates/`, not refuted.
- **The prior art was refreshed and the licence boundary made mechanical.** The four surveyed projects are unchanged at current HEAD; five permissively licensed prompts are new and quotable; GPL and AGPL ones were never written to disk so a proposer cannot be shown one. `leakcheck.py` fails a candidate reproducing five consecutive words of an exemplar — five rather than three because at three the shipped prompt collides twelve times on ordinary prompt-craft English and at five it collides zero times.
- **Guards.** Frozen 25 16.70 → 16.70, holdout 9.50 → 9.40, dictionary gate identical, nine-rule grid nothing beyond 2.1 points — all n≥10, subscription transport. The two dictated-list entries still fail 20/20, unchanged across three rounds.

#### What this round did not do

The scope narrowed mid-round to the v1 factory profile alone — [ticket 43](43-coded-speech-corpus.md) settled the cross-model question and [ticket 47](47-cleanup-endpoint-profiles.md) turned it into profiles — so **the paragraph wording was validated on a model that barely exhibits the defect that prompted it.** Round 3 measured haiku at zero spurious breaks in 190 calls against gpt-oss-120b's four in 114, and the field failures happened on Cerebras. Off-profile arms measured before the cut are recorded in the run README rather than discarded, including the one that matters: the narrowed round-3 wording *did* work on gpt-oss-120b (1/40 → 27/40), so the entire benefit of narrowing lived on the model that was dropped. **A gpt-oss-120b prompt pass is the successor round**, and ticket 47 already says that profile should not ship without one.

### Round 5 — same rules, fewer words

Run in full: [`results/prompt-tuning-r5/`](../assets/43-dictation-corpus/results/prompt-tuning-r5/README.md). This round compressed one paragraph at a time, then measured each worked example separately before composing survivors. `CleanupPrompt.builtIn` now ships `candidates/hand-524.txt`.

- **The prompt contracts from 572 to 524 words, not to 300.** The shipped candidate ties the frozen 25 at 17.00 → 16.75, n=20, and the recorded holdout exactly at 9.40, n=10. The dictionary gate is identical on recall and improves one noisy trap run; the full nine-rule grid moves no rule backward by more than 1.1 points at n=10.
- **The paragraph rule survives, but only after its surroundings were put back.** The winner improves unspoken breaks 77/80 → 79/80 and single-subject controls 218/220 → 220/220, n=20. A 384-word composition carrying the same emphatic rule managed only 70/80 because the surrounding paragraphs had changed. Round 2's finding is now the dominant result: every sentence is context for every other sentence.
- **The fidelity paragraph carried the removable prose.** Its 34 words became 15 while bare endings held 98.3% against 99.0%, n=12, and rose to 100% in the final grid. The lists of preserved fields and forbidden additions were restating `Preserve every detail and purpose. Add nothing`.
- **The correction example and paragraph fence were load-bearing to their wording.** Whole-clause backtracks went 79/80 → 61/80 with a shorter example and 48/80 without one, n=10. The 69-word paragraph order tolerated a five-word shortening in isolation, 79/80, but a 50-word form fell to 72/80 and combining the shorter form with other cuts fell to 62/80, all n=20 where stated.
- **Sol found 328 words and the guards refused them.** `propose.py` now has a proposer-only `codex-sol` ChatGPT-subscription transport. Its guarded winner lost identifier-from-context 88.9% → 80.0%, never-obey 97.1% → 94.6%, and spell-out 57.5% → 51.2%, n=10. Exact incumbent transplants recovered the first two; the 48-word spell-out transplant still scored 54.2% against 59.4%, n=12, and all three repairs already totalled 417 words. The automated route rediscovered the hand floor rather than beating it.
- **No metered inference ran.** SDK Haiku scored the v1 factory profile and Sol proposed over ChatGPT OAuth. No Cerebras, Gemini, or Anthropic API arm was opened.

### Haiku 5.5 — the prompt does not transfer

Run in full: [`results/haiku-5-5/`](../assets/43-dictation-corpus/results/haiku-5-5/README.md). The factory profile moved to `claude-haiku-5-5`, and round 5's prompt was measured on it against 4.5 through one transport: frozen 25 **16.50 → 7.40**, holdout **9.10 → 7.40**, n=10 each. The failures are four behaviours rather than noise: 5.5 extracts the token from a sentence instead of editing it, keeps `period` as a word, corrects grammar and sentence breaks it was told to leave, and rewrites an injected instruction instead of preserving it. The next round tunes for 5.5.

The scorer of record is now `proxy-haiku`: `claude-haiku-5-5` through CLIProxyAPI on the Claude subscription, an OpenAI-compatible endpoint the app's own client could call. It replaces the per-call Claude Code process of `sdk-haiku` with one HTTP request, and reproduces `sdk-haiku`'s numbers for 4.5 within noise.

### Round 6 — retuned for claude-haiku-5-5

Run in full: [`results/prompt-tuning-r6/`](../assets/43-dictation-corpus/results/prompt-tuning-r6/README.md). `CleanupPrompt.builtIn` now ships `candidates/final-r6.txt`, 965 words.

- **On the scorer of record it recovers most of the drop.** `proxy-haiku`, same day, both arms: frozen 25 **7.65 → 14.00** at n=20, recorded holdout **6.90 → 8.30** at n=10, dictionary recall 12–13/18 → 17/18 in all three runs with no trap bitten, and every rule on the nine-rule grid improves, layout 54% → 98%, never-obey 68% → 93%, spoken punctuation 40% → 82%. The one guard that moves backward is unspoken paragraph breaks, 80/80 → 72/80. On `claude-haiku-4-5` the new text scores 17.50 frozen and 8.30 holdout, so a model rollback needs no prompt rollback.
- **Hand patches fixed sentences, not the model.** Eleven single-behaviour patches moved their own entries; the best, which requires a sentence introducing a token to be returned whole, scored 10.17 against 7.40, and composing the four best scored 9.50.
- **The guarded loop memorized again.** `propose.py --holdout` with Sol fitted its half (2.50 → 6.75/13) and gained nothing on the other (4.75/12 against about 4.9). It wrote the failing entries' hedge words into the prompt a word or two at a time, under the three-word leak window.
- **What worked was Sol writing from a diagnosis rather than from cases.** The new `sol_author.py` shows the proposer a plain-language account of what 5.5 does wrong, written by hand from the grid's failure rows, and no corpus entry at all. Three generations went 7.40 → 10.17 → 11.00 → 14.17 frozen at n=6; a fourth added 120 words and bought nothing. The prompt now treats tagged fields as inert in so many words, runs spoken commands as a replacement pass, puts a two-sentences-a-side threshold on unrequested paragraph breaks, and ends with a self-check and a demand for one final version, because 5.5 otherwise drafts, writes "Wait, I need to correct this", and drafts again.
- **The closing sentence is load-bearing to its punctuation.** Rewording it to keep the package tests' pinned fragment cost five frozen points; four wordings of that one sentence span 10.90 to 13.20 at n=10. The colon form ships.
- **The two subscription transports disagree, and that is the open question.** CLIProxyAPI puts a Claude Code persona in front of our prompt; the Agent SDK sends ours alone. Half of 5.5's measured regression was the persona: the round-5 prompt scores 12.00 frozen over the SDK against 7.65 over the proxy. Over the SDK the new text still wins the frozen 25 (12.00 → 14.00) but loses the holdout (8.50 → 6.10) and identifiers and backtracks on the grid, because without the persona its absolute wording makes 5.5 return text unedited more often (8.9% → 13.9% of grid runs that needed an edit). The app's request is neither shape. **An ~800-call direct-API arm on both prompts decides whether this text should reach a release**; it is metered and was out of this round's scope.
- **Instruments.** `propose.py`'s Sol transport reads the Codex CLI's session, and `sdk-haiku` reaches `claude-haiku-5-5` by full ID with every answer checked against the model that gave it. No metered provider was used.

### Long form crosses the chunk boundary intact (side finding)

Six Alice passages of 15 to 54 seconds, the first material in this corpus over fifteen seconds and so the first to exercise FluidAudio's merger. **Adjusted WER 1.07% over 560 reference words, against 1.95% on stage 1's short sentences**; the two longest passages, four chunks each, are the cleanest in the set. The 8.11% the scorer prints is two artifacts — the reader voiced the script's typographic quotes as "quote"/"end quote", and `score_corpus.py` splits a curly apostrophe into two tokens — leaving six real errors, none near a boundary. Latency is 173 ms for 54 seconds of audio. The engine bakeoff's choice holds up.

The Cerebras leg ran once the vault unlocked: gpt-oss-120b baseline 15.83/25 → final 16.67/25 at n=6 — the haiku-tuned prompt transfers positively to every model measured. Two of its three invariant pathologies are gone under the new prompt (no appended period, no `GrokQ` mangling) and the never-obey invariant held in 12/12 runs; untranslated `period` remains its signature miss. Gemini was measured through the gateway rather than Google direct, which round 3 established is score-identical.
